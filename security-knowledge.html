OWASP APPLICATION SECURITY

Five important application risks

Broken Access Control A01

My understanding

Broken access control happens when a user can access information, permissions, or actions that they should not be authorized to use.

Why it matters

An attacker may access another account, elevate privileges, move laterally, or reach administrative functions and sensitive data.

How organizations reduce it

Use server-side authorization, RBAC, least privilege, Zero Trust principles, deny-by-default rules, and regular access reviews.

Injection A05

My understanding

Injection happens when an application treats malicious input as a trusted query or command, such as SQL or operating-system code.

Why it matters

It can expose, modify, or delete data, bypass authentication, interrupt a website, or execute unintended commands.

How organizations reduce it

Use parameterized queries, input validation, secure code review, least-privilege accounts, testing, and a WAF as an added layer.

Security Misconfiguration A02

My understanding

Security misconfiguration happens when an application, server, cloud service, or security tool is set up incorrectly.

Why it matters

Default credentials, unnecessary services, exposed storage, or detailed error messages can give attackers an easy path inside.

How organizations reduce it

Use hardened baselines, remove unnecessary services, patch systems, protect secrets, and continuously scan configurations.

Cryptographic Failures A04

My understanding

Cryptographic failures happen when sensitive information is not protected correctly while stored or transmitted.

Why it matters

Passwords, tokens, financial data, or company information could be exposed and used for fraud or account takeover.

How organizations reduce it

Encrypt data at rest and in transit, use modern standards, protect keys, rotate secrets, and securely hash passwords.

Logging & Alerting Failures A09

My understanding

Logging and alerting failures happen when an organization lacks the visibility needed to recognize and investigate suspicious activity.

Why it matters

Attackers may stay inside an environment longer, move laterally, steal data, and leave the security team without a clear timeline.

How organizations reduce it

Centralize logs, protect their integrity, create useful alerts, tune detections, define retention, and assign investigation ownership.

OWASP LLM SECURITY

Five important AI security risks

Prompt Injection LLM01

My understanding

Prompt injection happens when malicious instructions try to make an AI system ignore its intended rules or perform an unauthorized action.

Why it matters

An attacker may manipulate responses, expose internal information, or misuse tools connected to the AI application.

How organizations reduce it

Treat prompts and external content as untrusted, limit tool permissions, validate outputs, monitor abuse, and require approval for high-impact actions.

Sensitive Information Disclosure LLM02

My understanding

This happens when an AI reveals customer data, credentials, internal documents, system instructions, or other private information.

Why it matters

Exposed information can support phishing, fraud, account takeover, intellectual-property theft, or another attack.

How organizations reduce it

Limit data access, classify and mask sensitive information, enforce role-based permissions, and prevent restricted data from entering unapproved AI systems.

Improper Output Handling LLM05

My understanding

Improper output handling happens when another system automatically trusts AI-generated content as safe and accurate.

Why it matters

Malicious or incorrect output could be executed as code, sent to a database, displayed as unsafe HTML, or used to change system behavior.

How organizations reduce it

Validate, sanitize, encode, and structure AI output before another application uses it. High-impact code or commands should receive human review.

Excessive Agency LLM06

My understanding

Excessive agency means an AI system has more permissions or decision-making power than it actually needs.

Why it matters

A manipulated AI could send messages, access files, change accounts, delete information, or affect business operations.

How organizations reduce it

Follow least privilege, allowlist tools, separate permissions, use rate limits, monitor activity, and require approval before high-impact actions.

Data & Model Poisoning LLM04

My understanding

Data and model poisoning happens when someone manipulates the information used to train, update, or guide an AI system.

Why it matters

Poisoned data can cause misinformation, biased results, hidden backdoors, and poor decisions that employees may still trust.

How organizations reduce it

Verify data sources, restrict who can modify datasets, track changes, scan uploaded content, test model behavior, and approve major updates.

NIST & ISO FRAMEWORKS

Connecting security controls to business risk.

A cybersecurity framework gives organizations an organized way to manage risk instead of purchasing random security tools and hoping they cover every weakness. Frameworks help define priorities, responsibilities, evidence, and what should happen before, during, and after an incident.

NIST CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORK 2.0

Six functions that organize cybersecurity risk

GV

Govern

Establishes how cybersecurity decisions are made through policies, roles, risk tolerance, leadership oversight, and third-party expectations.

ID

Identify

Focuses on understanding systems, data, users, suppliers, vulnerabilities, and the risks that could affect the organization.

PR

Protect

Uses safeguards such as MFA, RBAC, encryption, firewalls, training, system hardening, and secure development practices.

DE

Detect

Uses logs, SIEM alerts, detection rules, endpoint monitoring, and analysis to identify suspicious activity as quickly as possible.

RS

Respond

Covers investigation, containment, communication, mitigation, documentation, and the actions taken during a cybersecurity incident.

RC

Recover

Restores systems and business operations, validates recovery, communicates progress, and applies lessons learned after an incident.

ISO/IEC 27001 & COMPLIANCE

How organizations manage and prove information security

Cybersecurity compliance to me means that an organization follows required standards, policies, laws, or contractual expectations and can prove that its controls are operating. Security tools alone are not enough because they can be misconfigured, ignored, or poorly managed.

Information Security Management System ISMS

An ISMS connects leadership, employees, policies, risk assessments, controls, evidence, audits, and improvement into one organized security-management system.

Risk Assessment RISK

A risk assessment identifies what could go wrong, which assets could be affected, how likely the event is, and how serious the business impact could be.

Risk Treatment PLAN

Risk treatment turns an identified risk into action. An organization may reduce, avoid, transfer, or formally accept the risk.

Asset Management ASSET

Asset management means knowing which devices, applications, cloud resources, accounts, and sensitive information the company owns, where they are, and who is responsible for them.

Access Control ACCESS

Access control makes sure users only receive the permissions required for their roles through authentication, authorization, MFA, least privilege, and regular access reviews.

Incident Management IR

Incident management defines how a company prepares for, detects, investigates, contains, communicates, documents, and learns from cybersecurity incidents.

Auditing & Improvement AUDIT

Auditing checks whether security controls work as claimed. Findings should lead to corrective actions, retesting, updated policies, and continuous improvement.

EXAMPLES OF SECURITY EVIDENCE

Policies, access reviews, authentication logs, training records, incident tickets, vulnerability reports, backup tests, security-tool configurations, audit findings, and corrective-action records.

This section demonstrates my personal understanding of these frameworks. It is not a claim of ISO certification or an official compliance assessment.
